# Digest: Lean Thinking and Agile Delivery Approaches

## Summary
This session introduced Lean management, which aims to increase efficiency in production by cutting lead time, response time, waste and work in process (WIP). It listed eight targets of the Lean approach. It then compared iteration-based Agile (equal-length sprints, similar-sized features, estimates by number of sprints) with flow-based Agile (work pulled as capacity allows, varying sprint time and effort, continuous delivery).

## Key concepts

### Lean Management
- Purpose: increase efficiency in production processes.
  - Reduce lead time in production.
  - Reduce response time from suppliers and customers.
  - Reduce use of resources that do not add value for the customer.
  - Reduce waste.
- Target: reduce the volume of work in process (WIP), and the wait time before work moves from one stage to another.

### Targets of the Lean Approach
- Eight targets: delay and waiting time; overproduction; overprocessing; non-value-adding activities; transportation; unnecessary movement or motion; excess inventory; defects in a product.
- Graph 1 (Lean principles cycle): "Lean" in the centre of six boxes: Eliminate Waste, Build Quality, Fast Delivery, Delay in Making Decisions, Empower Team, Optimize the Whole. Shows Lean as a set of connected principles.

### Iteration-Based Agile
- Sprints are all equal in time (time boxes; the student noted Kanban here).
- Features are all equal in effort (similar story points).
- Estimates are based on the number of sprints needed to produce the required features.
- Graph 2: four sprints of 20 SP each. Feature A is 40(20) SP and is split across Sprints 1 and 2; Feature B is in Sprint 3 and Feature C in Sprint 4. Each iteration takes the same time, iterations are the same length, and delivery cadence is consistent for all product increments.

### Flow-Based Agile
- Backlog work progresses as capacity allows.
- Sprint time varies; feature delivery effort varies.
- KPIs: WIP at each workflow stage, and cycle time.
- Delivery is continuous.
- Graph 3: Sprint 1 is wider and holds Feature A (40 SP) unsplit; Sprints 2 and 3 hold Features B and C (20 SP each). Time per feature differs, iterations differ in length, and delivery cadence varies.

## Background (not from notes)
- These points come from the note file's "added context" sections, not from class.
- Lead time is total time from request to delivery; WIP is work started but not finished. More WIP means longer waits between stages, so limiting WIP shortens lead time.
- Overprocessing: doing more work than the customer needs. Transportation: unnecessary movement of materials or information. Motion: unnecessary movement by people. Defects force rework. Excess inventory ties up resources.
- Lean software principle "delay decisions" means putting off irreversible decisions until the last responsible moment; this differs from idle waiting time as a waste.
- Fixed sprints are most associated with Scrum; Kanban is usually linked to flow-based work.
- Story points are a relative measure of size or difficulty. Cycle time is time from starting an item to finishing it.
