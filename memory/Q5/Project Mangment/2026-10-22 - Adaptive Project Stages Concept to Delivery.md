# Digest: Adaptive Project Stages: Concept to Delivery

*Project Mangment · 2026-10-22 · source: `classes/Q5/Project Mangment/2026-10-22 - Adaptive Project Stages Concept to Delivery.md`*

## Summary
Adaptive projects have three apparent stages: Concept, Construct and Deliver, and Close. The Concept stage produces a vision statement and a product roadmap. The Construct and Deliver stage breaks requirements into epics, features and user stories, plans releases, and uses routines such as daily standups, an iteration review and a retrospective.

## Key concepts

### Apparent Stages of Adaptive Projects
- Three stages: Concept; Construct and Deliver; Close.

### Concept Stage
- Vision statement: summarizes the project with a powerful phrase or short description; describes the best achievable outcome; creates a common, cohesive picture in team members' minds; inspires passion for the outcome.
- Product roadmap includes: a requirements document with tentative release dates; a stakeholder management plan; a team charter (how the team will perform its work).

### Construct and Deliver Stage
- Collect and decompose requirements into epics and user stories.
- Epic: large business goal spanning multiple sprints. Feature: distinct, deliverable capability inside an epic. User story: small, single-interaction task completed within a single sprint.
  - Example epic: launch a mobile e-commerce store (3 to 6 months, enables digital sales).
  - Example feature: express checkout system (marketable, shippable; 2 sprints).
  - Example user story: "As a returning customer, I want to use my saved credit card with one tap so that I can buy items quickly."
- Release planning: create a timetable to deliver in iterations.
- Create a prioritized product backlog.
- Estimate effort in hours or story points.
- Hold daily standups, an iteration review, and a retrospective to capture lessons learned.

## Background (not from notes)
- These points appear in the note only as added context, not from class.
- "Apparent" stages: adaptive projects iterate rather than follow strict one-way phases, so the stages are a loose guide.
- Release dates are tentative because requirements and priorities change as the team learns.
- A stakeholder management plan lists people affected by or interested in the project and how to engage them.
- Hierarchy runs epic > feature > user story; user stories often follow "As a [user], I want [action] so that [benefit]".
- A sprint is a short fixed-length cycle, commonly one to four weeks.
- Story points are a relative size/complexity/uncertainty measure.
- Standup: short daily meeting on progress and obstacles. Iteration review: shows finished work to stakeholders. Retrospective: team reflects on how to improve.
- Connection to class: the 2026-10-19 digest covers when to choose an adaptive approach and its environment.
