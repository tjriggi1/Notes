# Digest: Project Value, Quality, and Cost of Quality

*Project Mangment · 2026-10-15 · source: `classes/Q5/Project Mangment/2026-10-15 - Project Value, Quality, and Cost of Quality.md`*

## Summary
The session covered how project delivery performance is judged by value and by quality. Project value is total benefits minus total costs, and predictive and adaptive projects realize it at different times. Quality is managed through three processes and supported by tools such as fishbone diagrams, Pareto charts and control charts. The session also covered Six Sigma and the four categories of cost of quality.

## Key concepts

### Delivery Performance: Project Value
- Project value = sum of all project-related benefits minus sum of all project-related costs.
- Predictive projects deliver their greatest value after initial deployment; the challenge is predicting value and re-evaluating it as changes are proposed. The business case segments of the plan documents record this value.
- Adaptive projects deliver value throughout the lifecycle; each delivery cycle lets value expectations be adjusted.
- Business value often continues long after the project has ended.

### Delivery Performance: Project Quality Management
- Quality is the degree to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfills requirements.
- Quality management focuses on defining and measuring the performance level of each deliverable.
- Meeting time, cost and scope objectives means nothing if stakeholders are unhappy with deliverable quality.
- Three processes: plan quality management approach, manage quality, control quality.

### Quality Management Tools
- Management reviews, walk-throughs, inspections: direct examination by an objective evaluator.
- Cause-and-effect (fishbone/Ishikawa) diagrams: find the root cause of a problem.
- Pareto charts: histograms ordering issues from most to least frequent; identify the most critical problem areas.
- Control charts: centerline is the mean number of defects over the period; quality lines at 3 sigma on either side of the mean.

### The Six Sigma Quality Measure
- Measure of near-perfection with respect to requirements: fewer than 3.4 defects per million opportunities.
- A combination of philosophy and tools sponsored by the Council for Six Sigma Certification (sixsigmacouncil.org).

### The Cost of Quality (COQ)
- All costs incurred over the life of a product from preventing nonconformance, appraising for conformance, and failing to meet expectations.
- Prevention: design, implementation and maintenance of the quality management system.
- Appraisal: measuring and monitoring quality activities, including gathering data on actual quality.
- Internal failure: finding and correcting defects before the customer receives the product, including re-work.
- External failure: correcting defects found after the customer has the product.
