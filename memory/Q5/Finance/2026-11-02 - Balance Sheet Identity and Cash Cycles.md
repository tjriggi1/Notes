# Digest: Balance Sheet Identity and Cash Cycles

*Finance · 2026-11-02 · source: `classes/Q5/Finance/2026-11-02 - Balance Sheet Identity and Cash Cycles.md`*

## Summary
This session covered the basic balance sheet identity written with net working capital, and how to solve it for the firm's cash. It then defined the operating cycle (inventory period plus accounts receivable period) and the cash cycle (operating cycle minus the accounts payable period).

## Key concepts

### Basic balance sheet identity
- Net working capital + fixed assets = long-term debt + equity.
- Net working capital = cash + current assets - current liabilities.
- Cash = long-term debt + equity + current liabilities - current assets other than cash - fixed assets.

### The operating cycle and cash cycle
- Operating cycle has 2 components: inventory period and accounts receivable period. Operating cycle = inventory period + accounts receivable period.
- Cash cycle has 3 components: inventory period, accounts receivable period and accounts payable period. Cash cycle = operating cycle - accounts payable period.
- Takeaway in the notes: a longer payable period shortens the cash cycle; a longer inventory or receivable period lengthens it.

## Key terms
- **Balance sheet identity**: net working capital + fixed assets = long-term debt + equity.
- **Net working capital**: cash + current assets - current liabilities (formula from class).
- **Operating cycle**: inventory period + accounts receivable period.
- **Cash cycle**: operating cycle - accounts payable period.
- The inventory, receivable and payable periods, fixed assets, long-term debt, equity and current liabilities are used in class, but their definitions in the note are marked "added, not from class" (see Background).

## Background (not from notes)
- These points come from the note file's "added context" sections, not from class.
- Current assets in the NWC formula mean those other than cash (inventory, receivables), so cash is not counted twice.
- Rearranging the identity makes cash the balancing item: financing raised less what is spent on other assets.
- Inventory period: time to buy or produce inventory and sell it. Receivable period: time to collect from customers. Payable period: time taken to pay suppliers.
- A longer cash cycle means the firm needs more financing to run operations.
- Illustration: 60 days inventory, 30 days receivables, 40 days payables gives an operating cycle of 90 days and a cash cycle of 50 days.
- Fixed assets are long-lived assets (property, plant, equipment); long-term debt is borrowing due in over a year; equity is the owners' residual claim; current liabilities are obligations due within a year.
