## Summary
This session covered how Scrum uses timeboxing to cap the length of its events in a 4-week sprint, including the Sprint Retrospective and Sprint Review. It then introduced Kanban as a flow-based adaptive approach for unpredictable work. It closed by briefly describing Disciplined Agile and the Scaled Agile Framework.

## Key concepts

### Typical Timeboxing in Scrum
- Timeboxing sets a fixed maximum amount of time for an activity; it helps prevent unnecessary or wasted time.
- The slide outlines timeboxing guidelines for Scrum events in a 4-week sprint; each event gets a maximum duration to ensure focus and productivity.
- The sprint itself is timeboxed to one month or less.
- Sprint Retrospective: a chance for the Scrum team to inspect itself and plan improvements for the next sprint. Timebox: 45 minutes for every week of the sprint.
- Sprint Review: inspects the increment and adapts the product backlog if needed.

### Adaptive Approach: Kanban
- Kanban is a flow-based Agile approach.
- Suitable when workload or work requests are unpredictable, or when timeboxing or a sprint cadence is not predictable or not preferred.

### Other Popular Adaptive Approaches
- Disciplined Agile: teams are based on specialty skills and work together on a comprehensive delivery life cycle.
- Scaled Agile Framework: defines alignment, collaboration and delivery across large numbers of Agile teams at the team, program, enterprise and portfolio levels.

## Key terms
- **Timeboxing**: fixed maximum duration for an event or activity to keep focus and avoid wasted time.
- **Sprint**: fixed-length period of one month or less; timeboxed.
- **Sprint Retrospective**: team inspects itself and plans improvements for the next sprint; 45 minutes per sprint week.
- **Sprint Review**: inspects the increment and adapts the product backlog if needed.
- **Kanban**: flow-based Agile approach for unpredictable workloads or when sprints are not preferred.
- **Disciplined Agile**: specialty-skill teams working on a comprehensive delivery life cycle.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)**: alignment, collaboration and delivery across many Agile teams at team, program, enterprise and portfolio levels.

## Background (not from notes)
- These points come from the note file's "added context" sections, not from class.
- For a 4-week sprint, 45 min x 4 weeks = about 3 hours, matching the Scrum Guide maximum for a one-month sprint. Shorter sprints get proportionally shorter events.
- Increment: usable, finished work from the sprint. Product backlog: ordered list of everything that may be needed in the product. The Review shows stakeholders the increment; the Retrospective looks at the team's way of working.
- Kanban: items move across a visual board (e.g. To Do, In Progress, Done), usually with limits on work in progress; suits support or operations teams with random requests.
- Disciplined Agile is a toolkit for choosing and tailoring ways of working; SAFe is used when many Agile teams coordinate on shared goals, often in large organizations.
