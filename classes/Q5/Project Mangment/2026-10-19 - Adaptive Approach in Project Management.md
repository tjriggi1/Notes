# Adaptive Approach in Project Management

*Project Mangment · 2026-10-19*

> The session covered when to choose an adaptive approach, the categories of decision criteria, the T-shaped team structure, and the requirements of an adaptive project environment.

## When to Use the Adaptive Approach

Ask the following questions to decide whether an adaptive approach fits a project.

- Are the project requirements complex?
- Does the project involve risk?
- Is early feedback from customers needed?
- Is the organization receptive to the flexibility and risk tolerance that the approach requires?

## Decision Criteria Categories

- Product/Service/Result-based: specific attributes of the expected outputs that the project generates.
- Project-based: specific attributes of the project management.
- Organization-based: attributes of the organizational context of the project.

## Team Structure in Adaptive Projects

Adaptive teams are referred to as "T-shaped" teams. Each member is outstanding in certain primary skills. Each member also has broad capabilities in other skills.

## Requirements of the Adaptive Project Environment

- Adaptive mindset.
- Individuals and interactions over process and tools.
- Working software over comprehensive documentation.
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
- Responding to change over following a plan.
- Structure and culture of adaptive teams.
- Adaptive approaches favor a distributed management and leadership approach.
- In some situations, a project team may self-organize to complete a project.
- Instead of a designated project manager, someone within the team may serve as a facilitator. The facilitator enables communication, collaboration, and engagement.
- Limited team size and experienced team members.

## Takeaways

- Complex requirements, risk, a need for early customer feedback, and an organization open to flexibility and risk all point toward an adaptive approach.
- Decision criteria fall into three categories: product/service/result, project, and organization.
- Adaptive teams are T-shaped, combining deep primary skills with broad capabilities.
- Adaptive environments favor distributed leadership, small teams, and experienced members, and may be self-organizing.
