# Project Value, Quality, and Cost of Quality

*Project Mangment · 2026-10-15*

> The session covered how project delivery performance is judged by value and by quality. It also covered quality management tools, the Six Sigma measure, and the cost of quality.

## Delivery Performance: Project Value

Project value is the sum of all project-related benefits minus the sum of all project-related costs.

- Predictive projects deliver their greatest value after initial deployment.
- The challenge for predictive projects is predicting value and re-evaluating it as changes are proposed.
- The business case segments of the plan documents record this value.
- Adaptive projects deliver value throughout the lifecycle.
- Each delivery cycle in an adaptive project allows value expectations to be adjusted.
- The business value of a project often continues long after the project has ended.

## Delivery Performance: Project Quality Management

Quality is the degree to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfills requirements. Quality management focuses on defining and measuring the performance level of each project deliverable.

Meeting time, cost, and scope objectives means nothing to stakeholders if they are not happy with the quality of the deliverables.

- Processes for managing quality deliverables: plan quality management approach, manage quality, and control quality.

## Quality Management Tools

- Management reviews, walk-throughs, and inspections: direct examination of the work or product by an objective evaluator.
- Cause-and-effect diagrams (fishbone or Ishikawa diagrams): help find the root cause of a problem.
- Pareto charts: histograms that display issues from the most frequent to the least frequent. They help identify the most critical problem areas of a project.
- Control charts: the mean is calculated by totaling the number of defects across the entire time period and determining the mean. The mean is shown as the centerline of the chart.
- Control charts also have quality lines that mark the outside limits. These are set at 3 sigma (3 standard deviations) on either side of the mean.

## The Six Sigma Quality Measure

- Six Sigma is a measure of near-perfection with respect to requirements.
- It means fewer than 3.4 defects per million opportunities, which is nearly perfect.
- It is a combination of philosophy and tools sponsored by the Council for Six Sigma Certification (sixsigmacouncil.org).

## The Cost of Quality (COQ)

The cost of quality is defined as all costs incurred over the life of a product. These costs come from investing in preventing nonconformance to requirements, appraising the product or service for conformance to requirements, and failing to meet expectations.

- Prevention costs: the design, implementation, and maintenance of the quality management system to reduce defects and failures.
- Appraisal costs: measuring and monitoring activities related to quality, including gathering data about actual deliverable quality.
- Internal failure costs: the cost of finding and correcting defects before the customer receives the product or service, including re-work.
- External failure costs: the cost of correcting defects found after the customer has the product or service.

## Takeaways

- Project value is total benefits minus total costs, and it often continues after the project ends.
- Predictive projects deliver the most value after deployment, while adaptive projects deliver value throughout the lifecycle.
- Hitting time, cost, and scope targets is not enough if stakeholders are unhappy with deliverable quality.
- Quality is managed through three processes: plan, manage, and control.
- Fishbone diagrams find root causes, Pareto charts rank problems by frequency, and control charts show limits at 3 sigma.
- Six Sigma means fewer than 3.4 defects per million opportunities.
- The cost of quality has four parts: prevention, appraisal, internal failure, and external failure.
