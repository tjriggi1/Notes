# Scrum Timeboxing and Adaptive Agile Approaches

*Finance · 2026-10-29*

> This session covered how Scrum uses timeboxing to limit the length of its events in a 4-week sprint, including the Sprint Retrospective and Sprint Review. It then introduced Kanban as a flow-based adaptive approach and briefly described Disciplined Agile and the Scaled Agile Framework.

## Typical Timeboxing in Scrum

Timeboxing means setting a fixed maximum amount of time for an activity. In Scrum it keeps events focused and stops time from being wasted.

- Timeboxing helps prevent unnecessary or wasted time.
- The slide outlines timeboxing guidelines for Scrum events in a 4-week sprint. Timeboxing sets a maximum duration for each event to ensure focus and productivity. The sprint itself is timeboxed to one month or less.
- Sprint Retrospective
    - It is an opportunity for the Scrum team to inspect itself and create a plan for improvements to be put in place during the next sprint.
    - The timebox is 45 minutes for every week of the sprint.
- Sprint Review
- The Sprint Review inspects the increment and adapts the product backlog if needed.

*Added context (not from class):*

> For a 4-week sprint, the 45 minutes per week rule gives a Sprint Retrospective of about 3 hours (4 x 45 minutes). This matches the Scrum Guide, which sets the retrospective at a maximum of 3 hours for a one-month sprint. Shorter sprints get proportionally shorter events.

> The increment is the usable, finished work the team produced during the sprint. The product backlog is the ordered list of everything that may be needed in the product. The Sprint Review is where stakeholders see the increment and the backlog is updated based on what is learned. The Retrospective, by contrast, looks at the team's own way of working.

## Adaptive Approach: Kanban

- Kanban is a flow-based Agile approach.
- It is suitable for the following organizational conditions:
    - The workload or work requests are unpredictable.
    - Timeboxing or a sprint cadence is not predictable or not preferred.

*Added context (not from class):*

> In Kanban, work items move continuously across a visual board (for example To Do, In Progress, Done) instead of being grouped into fixed-length sprints. Teams usually limit how many items can be in progress at once. This suits teams such as support or operations teams, where new requests arrive at random.

## Other Popular Adaptive Approaches

- Disciplined Agile
    - Teams are based on specialty skills and work together on a comprehensive delivery life cycle.
- Scaled Agile Framework
    - It defines alignment, collaboration, and delivery across large numbers of Agile teams at the team, program, enterprise, and portfolio levels.

*Added context (not from class):*

> Disciplined Agile (DA) is a toolkit that helps teams choose and tailor ways of working for their own context. The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) is used when many Agile teams must coordinate on shared goals. It is often used in large organizations.

## Key terms

- **Timeboxing**: Setting a fixed maximum duration for an event or activity so the team stays focused and productive and avoids wasting time.
- **Scrum**: An Agile framework in which a team works in short, timeboxed sprints and uses defined events to plan, inspect and adapt its work. *(added, not from class)*
- **Sprint**: A fixed-length period of one month or less in which the Scrum team produces a usable increment of work. It is timeboxed.
- **Sprint Retrospective**: A Scrum event where the team inspects itself and creates a plan for improvements to carry out in the next sprint. The timebox is 45 minutes for every week of the sprint.
- **Sprint Review**: A Scrum event that inspects the increment and adapts the product backlog if needed.
- **Increment**: The usable, completed work produced during a sprint that adds to the product. *(added, not from class)*
- **Product backlog**: The ordered list of all the work, features and fixes that may be needed in the product. The Scrum team updates it as it learns more. *(added, not from class)*
- **Kanban**: A flow-based Agile approach that suits unpredictable workloads or situations where timeboxing or a sprint cadence is not predictable or preferred.
- **Adaptive approach**: An Agile way of working that responds to change and feedback instead of following a fixed plan. Scrum and Kanban are examples. *(added, not from class)*
- **Disciplined Agile**: An adaptive approach in which teams based on specialty skills work together on a comprehensive delivery life cycle.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)**: A framework that defines alignment, collaboration and delivery across large numbers of Agile teams at the team, program, enterprise and portfolio levels.

## Takeaways

- Timeboxing sets a maximum duration for events to keep them focused and prevent wasted time.
- A sprint is timeboxed to one month or less.
- The Sprint Retrospective is for the team to inspect itself and plan improvements, with 45 minutes per week of sprint.
- The Sprint Review inspects the increment and adapts the product backlog if needed.
- Kanban is flow-based and suits unpredictable workloads or teams that do not want sprints.
- Disciplined Agile and the Scaled Agile Framework are other popular adaptive approaches. SAFe is designed for large numbers of Agile teams.
